    ' v_summary: 'Black seed is one of the most valued remedies in Unani and Islamic prophetic medicine. Small human trials suggest modest effects on blood pressure, cholesterol and blood sugar, but most studies are short and the products used vary widely.' v_verdict: 'Reasonable as a food. As a supplement, effects are modest and best treated as an add-on, not a replacement for prescribed treatment.' v_uses: 'Blood pressureLimitedSeveral small trials report modest drops in mildly raised blood pressure.Cholesterol and triglyceridesLimitedPooled small studies show small improvements; quality is uneven.Blood sugar in type 2 diabetesLimitedSmall trials suggest a modest fall in fasting glucose and HbA1c alongside usual care.Asthma and allergiesInsufficientEarly studies only; not a substitute for inhalers.' v_research: 'Most research uses black seed oil or powdered seed in capsules for 8 to 12 weeks. Reviews of these trials find small average improvements in blood pressure, lipids and glucose markers, with wide differences between studies. Larger, longer, independently funded trials are still needed, and no study shows that black seed replaces medicines for these conditions.' v_safety: '
  • Food amounts are considered safe for most adults.
  • Capsules and oil can cause stomach upset, nausea or bloating.
  • Applying the oil to skin can cause allergic contact dermatitis; patch-test first.
  • It may lower blood sugar and blood pressure, so people on medicines for these should monitor closely.
  • Isolated reports link very high doses to kidney problems.
