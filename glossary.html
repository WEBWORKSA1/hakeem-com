Unani glossary

The vocabulary of Unani medicine, from the four humours to the names of classical preparations. Spellings vary between Urdu, Persian and Arabic sources; we use the forms most common in South Asia.

Ajnas-e-Ashra اجناس عشرہ
The ten signs a practitioner uses to judge temperament, including touch, build, hair, sleep and emotional disposition.
Akhlat اخلاط
The four humours: blood (dam), phlegm (balgham), yellow bile (safra) and black bile (sauda).
Arkan ارکان
The four elements of classical theory: earth, water, air and fire.
Arq عرق
A distillate, made by distilling herbs in water.
Asbab-e-Sitta Zarooriya اسباب ستہ ضروریہ
The six essentials of health: air; food and drink; movement and rest; sleep and wakefulness; retention and elimination; and emotions.
Balgham بلغم
Phlegm, the humour described as cold and moist. Gives the phlegmatic (balghami) temperament.
BUMS
Bachelor of Unani Medicine and Surgery, the main professional degree in India, regulated by the National Commission for Indian System of Medicine (NCISM).
CCRUM
Central Council for Research in Unani Medicine, India's government research body for Unani.
Dalak دلک
Therapeutic massage.
Dam دم
Blood, the humour described as hot and moist. A dominant dam gives the sanguine (damwi) temperament.
Daraja درجہ
Degree. Medicines are graded from first to fourth degree for how strongly hot, cold, moist or dry they are.
Dawakhana دواخانہ
A pharmacy where Unani medicines are prepared and sold.
Habb حب
A pill.
Hakeem (Hakim) حکیم
Literally 'wise one'. A practitioner of Unani medicine. In India and Pakistan the title is used by registered practitioners of the system.
Hijama حجامہ
Cupping, a regimenal therapy. Wet cupping involves small cuts. See our guide.
Ilaj bil Dawa علاج بالدوا
Pharmacotherapy: treatment with medicines, mostly plant-based.
Ilaj bil Ghiza علاج بالغذا
Dietotherapy: treatment through food.
Ilaj bil Tadbeer علاج بالتدبیر
Regimenal therapy: treatment through measures such as cupping, massage, exercise, baths and diet.
Ilaj bil Yad علاج بالید
Surgery (literally 'treatment by hand').
Itrifal اطریفل
A confection based on the three myrobalan fruits, including amla.
Joshanda جوشاندہ
A decoction made by boiling herbs in water; also the name of a popular cold remedy blend.
Khaisanda خیسانده
An infusion made by soaking herbs in water.
Khamira خمیرہ
A semi-solid confection, often whipped, used as a tonic.
Majoon معجون
A semi-solid preparation of powdered herbs mixed with honey or sugar syrup.
Matab مطب
A practitioner's clinic.
Mizaj مزاج
Temperament: the overall balance of hot, cold, moist and dry qualities in a person, organ or medicine. See the four temperaments.
Mu'tadil معتدل
Balanced or moderate temperament.
Mufarreh مفرح
An exhilarant: a medicine traditionally used to lift the spirits, such as saffron.
Nabz نبض
Pulse. Pulse reading is a central diagnostic skill in Unani practice.
Riyazat ریاضت
Exercise.
Roghan روغن
An oil, for example roghan badam (almond oil).
Safoof سفوف
A powder.
Safra صفرا
Yellow bile, described as hot and dry. Gives the choleric (safrawi) temperament.
Sauda سودا
Black bile, described as cold and dry. Gives the melancholic (saudawi) temperament.
Sharbat شربت
A syrup.
Su-e-Mizaj سوء مزاج
Dystemperament: an imbalance of temperament, the classical explanation of illness.
Tabiyat طبیعت
The body's own healing power (similar to the Latin vis medicatrix naturae), which treatment aims to support.
Tibb طب
The Arabic and Urdu word for medicine. Tibb-e-Unani means Greek (Ionian) medicine, the full name of the Unani system.
Unani یونانی
From Yunan, Greece. A system of medicine built on Greek foundations and developed by Persian and Arab physicians, now practised mainly in South Asia.
