Why it matters

Studies in many countries find that a large share of people who use herbal remedies do not tell their doctors. Common reasons are that nobody asked, that the remedy seemed harmless, or a worry about being judged. Yet herbs can affect blood tests, interact with prescriptions and complicate surgery. Your doctor can only weigh those risks if they know.

Before the appointment

  • Write a list of everything you take: prescription medicines, over-the-counter products, vitamins, supplements, herbal teas and traditional medicines from a hakeem or vaid.
  • For each, note the name, the dose and how often you take it. If you can, take photos of the labels or bring the containers.
  • Note why you take each herb and whether you think it helps.

A simple script

"I want to make sure everything I take works safely together. As well as my prescriptions, I take these herbal products. Could you check whether any of them could interact with my medicines or my condition?"

Framing it as a safety check, rather than asking for approval, keeps the conversation practical. If you are seeing a traditional practitioner as well, say so; good doctors would rather know.

Questions worth asking

  1. Could this herb interact with any of my medicines?
  2. Could it affect any of my blood tests?
  3. Should I stop it before surgery or a procedure, and when?
  4. Are there signs I should watch for?
  5. Is there a better-proven option for what I am using it for?

Use your pharmacist

Pharmacists are often the fastest and most accessible source of interaction advice, and many have access to professional herb–drug interaction databases. Ask them whenever you start a new medicine or herb.

If you feel dismissed

Some clinicians are more familiar with herbal medicine than others. If you feel brushed off, ask specifically about safety and interactions rather than effectiveness, ask your pharmacist, or bring printouts of the herb's profile from a reliable source. Our herb profiles list their sources so a clinician can check them quickly.

Telling your traditional practitioner about your medicines

The conversation goes both ways. A responsible hakeem or herbalist should ask about every prescription you take, and should never tell you to stop prescribed medicine. If they do, that is a warning sign.
