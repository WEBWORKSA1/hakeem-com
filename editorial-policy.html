Editorial policy

How we decide what to say about a herb, where our information comes from, how we fix mistakes, and how money is kept away from content.

Sources we rely on

We start with independent, publicly funded or peer-reviewed sources: the US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH), the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, MedlinePlus and LiverTox from the US National Library of Medicine, Memorial Sloan Kettering's About Herbs database, European Medicines Agency herbal monographs, Cochrane reviews, and systematic reviews and meta-analyses indexed in PubMed. For traditional use, we draw on classical Unani and Ayurvedic texts and official pharmacopoeias.

We do not use manufacturers' marketing, testimonials or single small studies as the basis for a claim of benefit.

How a profile is written

  1. We collect the main sources and list every use people commonly claim for the herb.
  2. Each use is graded on our five-level evidence scale.
  3. Safety, who should avoid the herb and medicine interactions are written before the benefits are summarised.
  4. Traditional use is written separately and labelled as tradition.
  5. A second editor checks every claim against its source before publication.

Medical review

We are building a board of licensed pharmacists and physicians. When a page has been reviewed by a board member, their name, credentials and the review date appear at the top of the page. Until then, pages show the date of our editorial review against cited sources.

Corrections

If we get something wrong, we fix it promptly and note significant corrections at the end of the page. Send corrections through our contact form. We aim to review every correction request within five working days.

Updates

Profiles are reviewed at least once a year, and sooner when a major new study or safety warning is published.

Advertising and sponsorship

  • Display advertising is served by Google AdSense. We do not choose individual ads, and ads do not imply endorsement. Ads are kept out of safety warnings and away from forms.
  • Sponsors (such as Adopt-a-Herb or contest sponsors) receive a labelled credit. They never see content in advance and cannot change it.
  • No company may sponsor the profile of a herb it sells.
  • Featured practitioners in the directory are labelled "Sponsored".
  • We do not publish paid articles or undisclosed affiliate links.

User contributions

Contest entries and reader stories are published as personal experience, separate from evidence sections, and are edited to remove medical claims.

Artificial intelligence

We may use software tools, including AI, to help draft and organise material. Every published claim is checked by a person against the cited source.
